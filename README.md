# HR Analytics: Employee Attrition Analysis
# Project Overview
This project delivers an end-to-end analysis of employee attrition for the Human Resources department, using real-world HR data to uncover the key drivers behind why employees leave the organization. The analysis combines data cleaning, data analysis, and visualize data into actionable business insights by building a dashboard.
# Objective
The primary goal of this project is to evaluate and understand employee attrition by examining its relationship with:
- Education
- Age
- Salary
- Years at the Company
- Job Role 
These insights aim to help HR stakeholders design targeted retention strategies and reduce turnover costs.
# Tools & Technologies
Power BI—Data cleaning, DAX measures, and interactive dashboard visualization
# Key Findings
- Education: Life Sciences has the highest attrition share (37%), followed by Medical (26%), Marketing (15%), Technical Degree (13%), and Other
  (5%).
- Age: Employees aged 26–35 have the highest attrition count, while 55+ has the lowest.
- Salary: Attrition is highest among the lowest earners and narrows as salary increases toward the 15K+ band.
- Tenure: Attrition peaks sharply in the first year, then tapers off through years 2–10, with smaller secondary peaks around year 5 and year 10 
- Job Role: Research Scientist has the highest attrition count (100), followed by Human Resources (58), Sales Representative (44), Laboratory Technician (31), and Manufacturing Director (5).
# Recommendations
- Build career development and growth plans for early/mid-career employees, who show the highest attrition.
- Consider salary adjustments or clearer pay-progression paths for entry-level roles.
- Dig into why research scientists and human resources roles attract most (workload, role clarity, management) and address root causes.
